## Why do lash extension prices vary so much?

Most of the difference comes down to five things.

1. **Handmade vs pre-made fans.** Pre-made fans come from a factory and go on fast, so the set takes less time. Handmade fans are made one at a time during your appointment, sized to each natural lash. They take longer, but the fans are lighter and fit your own lashes.
2. **Time.** A careful full set takes about 1.5 to 2.5 hours. A "full set" finished in 30 to 45 minutes usually means fewer lashes or pre-made clusters.
3. **Skill and qualifications.** A trained artist isolates every lash so your natural lashes never stick together. That is about the health of your own lashes, not just looks. Ying holds Thailand's national Eyelash Technician Level 3 qualification from TPQI ([see the certificate](/about)).
4. **Materials.** Glue and lash fibers come in many grades. Our glue is fast-curing, so you can shower 2 hours after your appointment.
5. **What the price includes.** Some studios charge extra for lower lashes, color, or fixes. At My Lash House, lower lashes are part of the set, and if lashes shed abnormally in the first 3 days because of the glue or the application, send us photos on Instagram and we retouch them free.

## A few hundred baht vs over a thousand: what is the difference?

A low price is not always a bad sign. Some studios run opening promotions or offer lighter sets for first-timers. If a price looks very low, ask three questions before you book:

- Are the fans handmade or pre-made?
- How long does the set take?
- If lashes fall out early, will they fix it?

Lashes that need an early refill or a removal are not really cheap. Add those costs together and they can come to more than a good set would have cost in the first place.

## How much should you spend on lash extensions?

Think in price per good-lash day, not price per appointment. A well-done set looks its best for 2 to 3 weeks before a refill. A set that sheds in the first week costs you more per day, however cheap it was.
